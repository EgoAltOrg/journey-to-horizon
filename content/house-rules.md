Journey to Horizon runs the published campaign frame almost exactly as written. A few things are done differently, and only for one reason: to make it work as a weekly, drop-in game with many GMs and no fixed group. Here they are.

## The four departures

1. **One level for the whole march.** Everyone shares a single level: the number of shrines the march has relit so far. Drop in at any table and you are the same level as everyone else, and a brand-new character joins at the march's current level. (This is what sets your [[terranamancy|Wellspring points]].)
2. **Your own Vessel of Light.** You carry your own consecrated Vessel rather than a party sharing one, so you are never tied to a single group and there is no "who is holding it this week" problem.
3. **Fast travel, backwards only.** You can jump instantly between shrines the march has already relit, but never into unexplored ground: the journey still has to be walked the first time. To jump, be inside a relit shrine with an Aurelian priest to perform the rite; it then carries the whole circle at once, for free.
4. **One shared map.** All the GMs build a single continent-wide map together, so the world stays consistent no matter whose night it is.

## The Rekindling

Two kinds of night make up the campaign:

- **Interludes** are the normal Wednesday hexcrawl: roaming, visiting sanctuaries, taking on local trouble, and using the *Locate Soul Echoes* move to find where the next Echo sleeps and what guards it.
- **A Rekindling** is the march-wide event to reclaim an Echo once a party has found it. The whole march is called, everyone plans the assault together out of session, then converges for one big set-piece.

An Echo's shrine is always held by one of three things, a single Shadowblight life-cycle plus the Cult:

- A **Shadowblight-infected sanctuary**, delved like a dungeon, with a newborn Colossus gestating at its heart.
- A **roaming Shadowblight Colossus**, what that newborn becomes if left too long, marching on a sanctuary to devour its Light.
- A **Cult-held sanctuary**, an occupied stronghold to storm.

**The longer the march waits, the worse it gets:** an ignored infected sanctuary hatches its Colossus, and a roaming Colossus that reaches a sanctuary destroys it. The same Echo grows harder to reclaim the longer it is left.

Nothing summons a Rekindling: **the players call it themselves**, deciding together when they are ready. Turnout is open and the fight is scaled to whoever shows, with no cap, so more players just means a bigger beast and more to do. You pick your own front: topple the legs, climb to the head, or hold the wall against the aberrations. Terranamancy is suppressed for the whole fight (the contested Wellspring is choked). Winning relights the shrine, reconnects it to the Wellspring, and **levels the whole march**; ten lit shrines open the road to Horizon. Losing does not destroy the Echo, it just escalates the threat guarding it.

Committing the whole march to one Rekindling leaves the rest of the world thin, which is exactly when the Cult strikes somewhere undefended. It is a real choice, not a free win.

## At the table

- **Rest:** one short rest a session; a long rest happens only at a sanctuary. Sessions start and end somewhere safe, so attrition resets each night.
- **The Doom Track** is marked once per active week (any week with at least one session), not once per session.
- **Exploration** is pure canon: you choose where to go next, and the map fills in as you explore.
